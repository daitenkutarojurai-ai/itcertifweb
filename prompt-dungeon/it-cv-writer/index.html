1. Ruthless CV review (start here)

First-pass audit. Tells you what to cut before you waste time rewriting.

Claude 4.7 Opus (2026-05)GPT-5 (2026-05)
Act as a tech recruiter who reads 50 CVs a day for <ROLE_TYPE> roles (e.g. DevOps Engineer, Cloud Architect, SOC Analyst).

Review this CV. Be brutally honest — no diplomatic softening, no 'good but'. Recruiters spend 7 seconds per CV; tell me what loses me at second 4.

<PASTE_YOUR_CV_TEXT>

Give me, in order:

1. **First 3 things a recruiter sees** — quote them verbatim. Score each /10 for impact.
2. **The buzzwords killing me** — list any of: "passionate", "team player", "detail-oriented", "hard-worker", "results-driven", "synergy". Quote where I used them.
3. **The achievements that are actually responsibilities in disguise** — anything starting with "Responsible for" or "In charge of". Quote each one.
4. **The 3 lines I should DELETE** (no rewrite, no improvement — just delete).
5. **The 1 missing thing that would 2x my callback rate** for <ROLE_TYPE>.

No intro, no conclusion. Just the 5 sections.
TipPaste the plain text only — no formatting, no fluff. If the model refuses to be harsh, prompt back: 'Less polite. I'm here for the diagnosis, not validation.'

2. Rewrite a bullet point as a STAR-format achievement

When you have responsibility-as-bullet ("In charge of monitoring") and need to turn it into an impact-as-bullet ("Cut nightly alert noise 70% by tuning Prometheus alertmanager routes").

Claude 4.7 Opus (2026-05)GPT-5 (2026-05)
Rewrite this CV bullet from responsibility-mode to impact-mode using the STAR pattern (Situation-Task-Action-Result) compressed into a single line.

Original bullet:
<PASTE_BULLET>

Context I can give you:
- What problem this work solved: <PROBLEM>
- What I actually did (tools/steps): <ACTIONS>
- What changed because of it (numbers if possible): <RESULT>

Give me 3 rewrites:
1. **The conservative version** — facts only, no embellishment.
2. **The recruiter-bait version** — leads with the metric, names the tool, hints at scale.
3. **The interview-bait version** — slightly vague on detail so the recruiter is forced to ask me about it in the interview.

Max 25 words per bullet. No 'leveraged', 'spearheaded', 'utilized'. Active verb, number, tool, business impact — in that order.
TipIf you don't have a number, the result is too soft. Force yourself to invent a *defensible* one ("reduced manual ops by ~10h/week") rather than skip — but only if you can justify it in an interview.

3. Optimize a CV for ATS parsing (without keyword-stuffing)

When you're applying through Workday / Greenhouse / Lever and your CV gets filtered out before a human ever sees it.

Claude 4.7 Opus (2026-05)GPT-5 (2026-05)
I'm submitting this CV through an ATS (Applicant Tracking System). Help me make it parseable without making it look spammy to a human.

My CV:
<PASTE_CV>

Job description I'm targeting:
<PASTE_JOB_DESCRIPTION>

Give me:

1. **Hard-skill keywords from the JD that are missing from my CV** — list them and tell me, for each, whether I plausibly have the skill (so I can add it honestly) or not (so I shouldn't).
2. **Section headers that ATS parsers expect** — tell me if mine are non-standard (parsers fail on creative headers like 'My Journey' or 'Tech Toolbox').
3. **Formatting red flags** — tables, columns, headers in images, fancy bullets — anything that destroys ATS parsing.
4. **A keyword density check** — tell me which JD skills appear 0 times, 1-2 times (good), or 5+ times (stuffed, will look spammy).
5. **A surgical rewrite** of my CV's top 5 bullets to naturally hit the missing JD keywords without copy-pasting JD language.

Rule: never add a skill I haven't claimed. If something's missing and I plausibly have it, flag it; don't fabricate.
TipDon't keyword-stuff. ATS parsers have anti-spam scoring too. Aim for natural distribution: each hard skill 2-3 times across the CV, not 8.

4. Generate 5 LinkedIn headline variants

When 'Software Engineer at <Company>' is your headline and you wonder why no recruiter reaches out.

Claude 4.7 Opus (2026-05)GPT-5 (2026-05)
Generate 5 LinkedIn headline variants for my profile. The headline appears in search results — recruiters search and pick whoever's headline answers their search query best.

My details:
- Current role: <ROLE>
- Years of experience: <YEARS>
- Top 3 hard skills: <SKILL_1>, <SKILL_2>, <SKILL_3>
- Type of role I want to be recruited for: <TARGET_ROLE>
- Industries I'm open to: <INDUSTRIES>
- One credential or specialty that differentiates me: <CREDENTIAL>

For each variant, name the recruiter persona it targets (e.g. "hiring DevOps in fintech") and the search keywords it ranks for.

Constraints:
- 220 character max per headline.
- No emojis at the start (LinkedIn search ranking penalty).
- No "open to work" wording in the headline itself (use the badge instead).
- Active phrasing — "I do X" not "X enthusiast".
- At least 2 hard skills explicit in the headline.
TipPick the headline whose target recruiter persona matches the jobs you want to be offered, not the jobs you currently have.

5. Decide if a cover letter is worth writing

Most tech cover letters are deleted unread. This prompt tells you when to skip and when it's actually worth the 30 minutes.

Claude 4.7 Opus (2026-05)
Help me decide whether to write a cover letter for this application.

Job description: <PASTE_JD>
Company size: <STARTUP | SCALEUP | ENTERPRISE | FAANG>
Application channel: <COMPANY_PORTAL | LINKEDIN_EASY_APPLY | EMAIL_TO_RECRUITER | REFERRAL>

Answer 3 questions:

1. **Will it be read?** (yes/no/maybe + reason — e.g., 'enterprise + portal apply → ATS routes you to recruiter who skims; cover letter ignored').
2. **Would it MATERIALLY change my chances?** (only yes if either: I have a non-obvious story that makes me fit, OR I have a personal connection to the company).
3. **If I write one, what's the ONE point it needs to make?** (one sentence — not a 4-paragraph essay).

If the answer to #2 is no, tell me what to do with that 30 minutes instead (e.g., 'reach out to the hiring manager on LinkedIn with a 3-line note about the specific feature mentioned in the JD').
TipFor 90% of tech applications, the cover letter is wasted effort. This prompt protects your time.
