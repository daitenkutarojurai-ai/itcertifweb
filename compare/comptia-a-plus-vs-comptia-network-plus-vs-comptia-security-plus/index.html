Who is each one for?

CompTIA A+ (220-1101/1102)

CompTIA · 220-1101 + 220-1102
You're starting IT from zero and targeting a first support / helpdesk job.
  • The ultimate entry point into IT: no prerequisites, designed for true beginners.
  • Covers the practical side of support — hardware, OS, mobile, troubleshooting — useful from your first helpdesk job.
  • Recruiters recognize it as proof that you can fix and configure a machine.
  • An essential foundation before Network+ if you've never opened a PC or managed a workstation.
  • Two exams to pass (Core 1 + Core 2) — the total cost climbs fast (~$500).
  • Very broad but shallow: it skims many topics without mastering any.
  • Tier-1 support salaries plateau quickly — it's a springboard, not a destination.
  • Valued less in Europe than in the US, where helpdesk is a huge market.

CompTIA Network+ (N10-009)

CompTIA · N10-009
You want to understand networking in depth before targeting the CCNA or security.
  • The vendor-neutral networking foundation: TCP/IP, the OSI model, subnetting, VLANs, routing — the base for everything else.
  • The perfect bridge before the CCNA if you want to specialize in Cisco networking.
  • Deeper than A+ while staying approachable — a good effort-to-recognition ratio.
  • An implicit prerequisite for security: you can't secure a network you don't understand.
  • A single but dense exam — subnetting trips up many candidates.
  • Vendor-neutral = general; an all-Cisco employer will often prefer the CCNA directly.
  • Less 'sellable' on its own on a résumé than Security+, which is in very high demand today.
  • Little graded hands-on practice — it's multiple choice, not live configuration.

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-701)

CompTIA · SY0-701
You're targeting cybersecurity (SOC, analyst, compliance) and already have networking basics.
  • The most in-demand entry-level cybersecurity cert — it appears in most junior SOC job postings.
  • DoD 8570/8140 approved: a must for US defense / contractor roles.
  • The best starting salary of the trio — security pays better than support or pure networking.
  • A direct springboard to CySA+, PenTest+ or cloud security certs (SC-200, AWS Security).
  • Assumes solid networking basics — taking it without Network+ (or equivalent) hurts.
  • Broad and conceptual: lots of vocabulary, little graded hands-on work.
  • The junior cyber market is saturated with certified candidates who lack experience.
  • Renewal every 3 years (CEUs or retake) — ongoing upkeep required.
